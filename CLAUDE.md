- Video flow (default, voice=elevenlabs): same four-shot walkaround prompt, but the user first approves an ElevenLabs narration for the car (Narration panel). Its MP3 is sent as Seedance `audio_urls` (promptNative mode 'speech', speaking window = narration length) to guide voice, words and pace; a long silent end made Seedance pad by repeating lines (the price said twice), so 30-second previews are read slower (`narrationVoiceSettings`, config `narration_speed`, default 0.9), the prompt says each sentence exactly once, and when 3 s or more remain after the speech the four presenter shots end with the speech and one or two camera-only pan shots of the car (nobody on screen or speaking) fill the rest; the final video KEEPS Seedance's own speech because its lips match it (user verdict after testing: swapping in the ElevenLabs MP3 made lip sync very bad). Captions are timed from the video's own audio. The swap code remains behind a per-job `audio_swap` flag. Words Seedance mispronounces are respelled inside the spoken line (`SEEDANCE_RESPELL`, overridable with config `respell`; San Ruh-mone was still wrong in the first test, default is now San Rah-moan; next fallback San Ra-MOAN, then drop the city from the spoken line); captions keep the real spelling. Captions: `captionCues` splits at sentence ends and commas and matches audio pauses to those boundaries (`captionAnchors`, DP); the car name keeps up to three trim words after it. Seedance often adds or repeats words ("a Subaru X", the price twice), which made script-timed captions drift and linger; so when config `whisper_cli`/`whisper_model` are set, `goldenHeardWords` runs local whisper.cpp (free, ~40 s) on the video's speech and `captionSpokenScript` aligns it to the script (captions always show exactly the approved caption text; words she added are marked CAPTION_EXTRA and used only for timing, never shown) before `captionCues` times it. Whisper's own word times are too loose to use directly; the pause spans do the timing. No whisper or <60% matched → the written script is timed as before. Spoken text says years and comma numbers as words (`numberSpeech`); `speechWordCount` keeps counting them as written so the tuned 55–60 word budget is unchanged.
